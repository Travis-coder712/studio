Topic 3 Through the lens of demand

Demand has been flat for a decade — could it double?

Operational demand — the electricity the grid must serve, after rooftop solar is netted off behind the meter — has barely moved from ~177 TWh since 2020. But that flatline hides real growth: rooftop quietly added ~16 TWh of behind-the-meter supply (2020→2025), soaking up underlying growth of ~7% so the grid never saw it. That truce is ending — EVs, electrification and data centres pull up. Whether demand grows ~60% or roughly doubles by 2050 is a range of outcomes, not a certainty. Shape matters as much as size; the calculator below builds the duck curve live.

Reading the trend
  • Operational demand was flat (~177–181 TWh) from 2020 to 2025 — but behind-the-meter rooftop grew from 13 → 29 TWh (+16 TWh) and efficiency did the rest, so underlying use actually rose ~7% while the grid saw none of it.
  • Step Change lifts it to ~290 TWh by 2050 (+62%); the scenario fan runs 244–433 TWh — i.e. anywhere from +38% to ~2.4×. The flat line is ending; the whole game is the slope.
  • The open question: can rooftop keep adding ~2.7 GW/yr fast enough to keep masking the new load? Rooftop has added ~3 TWh/yr; EVs + electrification alone add ~4–5 TWh/yr through the 2030s — so the masking effect starts to lose the race.

◆ What demand does to price — a hand-off to the Market-cycles team

Demand growth isn't just a volume story — it moves the whole price curve, which is exactly what Topic 1 (Market cycles) trades on. Here's the rule of thumb, straight from the data.

Rule of thumb · demand → price (all else equal)

In the normal range, each +1 GW of average demand ≈ +$20/MWh. But the relationship is convex — into scarcity, every extra GW costs far more. NSW daily average, 2025–26:

~6 GW$64/MWh
~7 GW$83/MWh
~8 GW$105/MWh
~9 GW$246/MWh

So EV, electrification and data-centre load that lift average demand a few GW push the pool price up ~$20–40/MWh — and via the returns-leverage panel in Topic 1, that's several points of project IRR. Put simply: demand growth is a tailwind to the price cycle; rooftop growth is the headwind. Which one wins, and when, is the question to hand back to the room.
