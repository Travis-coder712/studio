Topic 2 Increasing renewable penetration in the NEM

Increasing renewable penetration in the NEM

Storage is critical — and the volume you need climbs steeply, not linearly, as wind and solar head toward 100%. The NEM is already ~44% renewable (grid wind+solar ~29%, plus hydro and rooftop) and spilling 7+ TWh/yr. Play the calculator first: set a wind + solar target, find the mix and the gas balance that keep storage sane.

Model & assumptions
A deterministic simulated NEM year at hourly resolution (~184 TWh demand, ~21 GW average / ~28 GW peak, dual summer/winter peaks; solar as a daily bell with seasonal swing, ~32% capacity factor; wind auto-correlated at ~34% CF with four multi-day lulls seeded in). These are potential (unconstrained) CFs — realised NEM CFs today are lower (wind 28%, solar 15%) because of curtailment and older plant. Wind+solar is built to overbuild × target of annual demand; the remaining (1 − target) is firm gas (standing in for today's coal + gas + hydro). A 4-hour battery fleet (power = energy ÷ 4) charges on surplus and discharges on deficit to time-shift renewable energy into the gaps; surplus beyond storage is spilled. The tool finds the minimum storage that lets delivered renewables reach the target — i.e. holds gas to its (1 − target) energy budget. As the target nears 100% that budget → 0, so storage must bridge multi-day lulls a 4-hour battery can't hold, and the model flags it. “Big battery” = 500 MW / 2 GWh. CFs are consistent with the AEMO-derived Lens figures; a stylised teaching model for the shape of the answer, not a capacity-expansion study.

◆ Context — who sets the price (secondary)

A decade of NEMDE price-setting attribution — how often each fuel set the 5-minute price. It's a secondary story here, but it's the flip side of the calculator: as storage does more of the firming, it also increasingly sets the price.

Share of 5-min energy price-setting intervals by fuel — % (2015 → 2026 YTD)
Reading the trend
  • Storage set the price 0.1% of intervals in 2015; in 2026 it's 31.5% — as often as all fossil generation combined, which fell from 78% to 31%.
  • As coal exits, the marginal price is increasingly whatever a battery will charge or discharge at — so the arbitrage spread becomes the price signal that funds (or starves) the last mile of storage.
